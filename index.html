    Home / Patterns

    The record

    Ornaments recovered from museum collections and from the makers themselves, written down so they can be worked again. Each one names the object it came from and where it was found.

    What a record is

    Every ornament is written down twice: as a chart a maker can work from, and as a vector drawing with its source beside it - the object, the collection, the region, the motif and what it meant. The description follows the format European archives use, so a record can leave this site without losing where it came from. Relief models of the carved and woven records are in preparation.

    Licensing

    Designers, architects and brands can license a record for their own work - printed cloth, tile, panelling, a whole collection. The maker who carries that ornament is named in the licence and paid from it, and a fixed part goes to the preservation fund. Tell us what you are making and we will come back with terms.

    About KATUN

    Somewhere in every country there is a person who still knows how a thing was made — a pattern, a glaze, a way of forging or carving that nobody wrote down. Almost none of it was written down. The knowledge lived in hands, and it ends when the hands stop.

    KATUN exists to keep the making alive, not just the memory of it. We find the object — in a regional museum, in a family chest, with the last person still working that way — record its ornament and where it comes from, and put it back into production with a maker who can carry it.

    The record is exact: the object, the collection, the region, the motif. The piece is not. A maker reads the record through their own hands, changes the scale, the material, sometimes the composition — and we say on every page what they changed. That is how this knowledge always travelled. A copy belongs under glass; a reading belongs in use.

    It started with textile in Montenegro, because that is where we live and where the first record came from. It doesn't end there. Clay, metal, wood, weaving, embroidery — different materials, the same method: find it, record it, make it again.

    Two things follow from that. The record does not stay on this site: it is kept as a drawing with its source attached, and designers, architects and brands can license it, with the maker who carries the ornament named and paid every time. And a fixed part of everything, sale or licence, goes into a preservation fund that buys material ahead, pays for the recording, and helps restore the objects we worked from.

    We're not a marketplace. We choose what goes on this site, we know every maker by name, and we take care of everything that isn't making: the photography, the writing, the translation, the shipping, the buyer. The maker makes. That's the whole arrangement.

    Home /The fund

    Heritage Preservation Fund

    Craft does not survive on orders alone. Part of what this site earns goes back into the making itself.

    A tenth of our own share on everything sold here, and a fifth of every licence fee, goes into a fund we keep apart from our own money. It comes out of our share, never out of the maker's: a maker's 65% is untouched by it.

    What it pays for

    • Raw material bought ahead - wool, thread, clay, glaze - so a workshop can work through a winter without waiting on an order.
    • The recording itself: travel to a collection, permission and photography, and drawing the chart from the object.
    • Restoration of an object we recorded, agreed with the museum that keeps it.
    • Teaching: paying an older maker for the time it takes to teach a younger one.

    Where it stands

    The fund is at zero. Nothing has been sold yet, so nothing has gone in. Once it starts, we publish once a year what came in and what it paid for, naming every workshop and museum it reached. Until then there is nothing to report, and we would rather say that than round it up.
